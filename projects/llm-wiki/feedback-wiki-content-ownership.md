Wiki content — especially pages on IP Fabric product direction, strategy, AIM, competitive positioning, continuous discovery, change assurance, and future scenarios — represents Daren's personal interpretation of practitioner and market needs, not confirmed roadmap or official company position, unless the page explicitly states it is roadmap or describes something already shipped.

**Why:** The distinction has not always been clear enough in how wiki content is framed when cited or applied. Daren's thinking about where IP Fabric *should go* is informed by deep experience and market reading, but is his own view — not IP Fabric's official position.

**How to apply:** When referencing wiki content in any context (planning sessions, Confluence drafts, conversations, presentations), frame it as "Daren's read on where this should go" or "based on practitioner needs as I understand them" rather than "IP Fabric is planning to..." or "IP Fabric's direction is...". Only treat content as confirmed when it explicitly says so (e.g. citing a shipped feature, a published roadmap item, or a confirmed engineering decision). This applies equally when the wiki content is used as input to external-facing materials — see also [[feedback-external-confidentiality]].
